ArtCurato turns the art and finds already in your photo library into a quiet, personal gallery. Everything runs on your device, and your photos are only ever read, never changed or uploaded.

How your gallery is organized

ArtCurato sorts your photos into two collections, based on where you took them, and puts both on a map.

Museums holds the art you photographed inside museums. Each museum you've been to gets its own page. Inside it, your photos are grouped by visit, the day you were there, and sorted into Art at the museum (the pieces themselves), Art with people (you or a companion beside a piece), and the building. A museum can also keep a few highlights, the masterpieces worth remembering.

Finds holds the street art, sculpture and landmarks you came across, never planned: a mural, a statue, a monument, a grand old cathedral. These are gathered by the city you found them in. Within a city they're grouped by place, named the way you'd say you went there: a sculpture garden, a temple, a park, rather than the street it's on. Every photo you've taken at one place sits together, however many times you've been back, and a place you returned to says so in its heading, with the years and the number of photos. If a name isn't right, type your own in the photo's Place field and it stays.

Map shows everything at once: every museum and find, pinned across the world.

The short version: Museums › each museum › your visits › the art, Finds › each city › each place › your finds, and Map for all of it.

Where the images come from

The images in ArtCurato come from three places:

  • Your own photos. Almost everything you see is a photo you took yourself, read from your photo library. It stays on your device, and the app never uploads or keeps a copy of it.
  • Public-domain masterpieces. The "Masterpiece of the day" and the paintings that open your gallery wall are famous works whose copyright has expired. Their images come from Wikimedia Commons and Wikipedia, and the app deliberately limits this to public-domain art.
  • Museum pictures and logos. A museum's page may show a banner picture from the museum's own website or from Wikipedia, and its logo from its website icon, to help you recognize the place. These belong to their owners. Your own photo replaces the banner whenever you choose one.

If you own an image shown here and would like it credited or removed, or believe one is shown in error, email us at artcuratoapp@gmail.com and we'll take care of it promptly.

Common questions

Do I need to import my photos? No. ArtCurato scans your library by itself, once you've allowed photo access, and files what it finds into Museums and Finds on its own. You can Stop a scan at any time (everything found so far is kept) and Resume later; it picks up where it left off. The scan is deliberately careful and only files what it's sure of, so if it passed over a real find, use Add Photos the Scan Missed in the + menu to file it by hand.

Can I see a photo up close? Yes. Tap the expand button in the corner of any photo, or tap the photo itself on its own page, and it opens full screen on a dark wall. Pinch to zoom, double-tap for a closer look, drag to move around. This works for your own photos and for the Masterpiece of the Day.

Can ArtCurato tell me what a piece is? Often, yes. When you open a photo of an artwork, the app reads the museum's wall label in your photos, either in the same shot or in a photo of the label you took around the same time, and fills in the title, artist, year and medium. The reading happens on your device. When it's clear, the details are saved for you; tap Not right? to undo them, or Edit to fix them. If there's no label to read, Search with Google Lens hands the photo to the Google app through the share sheet, and only when you tap it.

How long does the first scan take? It depends on the size of your library. A few thousand photos go quickly. A library of tens of thousands can take a few hours the first time, because every photo is examined on your own device rather than sent to a server. You can keep using the app while it runs, and you can Stop whenever you like and Resume later; nothing found so far is lost. After that first pass, each launch only looks at photos added since, which takes moments. You don't need to watch it, either: leave your iPhone charging overnight and the scan finishes on its own.

Do I have to keep the app open while it scans? No. Close the app and scanning carries on by itself: overnight, while your iPhone charges. That's deliberate. ArtCurato waits until your phone is plugged in, so the overnight scan draws on the charger, not your battery. Open the app the next morning and your gallery is caught up. One note: if you force-quit the app (swiping it away in the app switcher), iOS pauses this until you next open it.

Does scanning use battery? The overnight scan doesn't: ArtCurato only scans in the background while your iPhone is plugged in, so it never draws on your battery. If you keep the app open to watch a first scan of a large library, that works like any busy app and uses some power while it runs. The easy answer is the one the app suggests: close it, charge overnight, and wake to a gallery that's caught up.

What's on the Map? Every museum and find with a location, pinned across the world. Museums are the labeled pins; finds are the small dots. The chips at the top filter the map (All, This year, Visited, Want to go, Finds), and each chip's dot matches its pin color. Tap a pin to open that museum or find. Add a museum to Want to go and it appears here too, so the map shows where you've been and where you're headed.

Does this need location on my photos? Yes. ArtCurato places your photos using the location your camera saved inside each one, so it works only for photos that have location information. A photo taken with location turned off, or one whose location was later stripped, can't be matched to a museum or city and won't appear.

Turn on location for the Camera (so new photos are geotagged):

  1. Open the Settings app (the gray gear icon).
  2. Tap Privacy & Security.
  3. Tap Location Services (near the top).
  4. Make sure the Location Services switch at the very top is green. If it's gray, tap it once to turn it green.
  5. Scroll down the list of apps and tap Camera.
  6. Under "Allow Location Access," tap While Using the App (a checkmark appears next to it).
  7. Make sure Precise Location below it is green, so the exact spot is saved.

Now open the Camera app and take photos as usual. Each one will include its location whenever your phone has a signal. (Deep indoors with no signal, a photo may still come out without one.)

Add location to a photo you already took:

  1. Open the Photos app and open the photo.
  2. Swipe up on the photo, or tap the info button, the small circled “i” (ⓘ) below the photo.
  3. Tap Add Location if there's none yet, or Adjust to change it.
  4. Type the place in the search box and tap the correct result.

To fix several at once: in an album tap Select, tap the photos you want, tap the More button (the three dots •••), then tap Adjust Location and set the place.

Keep location when you share or AirDrop a photo:

  1. Tap the Share button.
  2. At the top of the share sheet, tap Options.
  3. Turn Location on, then tap Done and share as usual.

If Location is off here, the shared or saved copy loses its location and won't be placed in ArtCurato.

Why don't I see all my visits? Two things matter most: the photo needs location information (see above), and the app needs access to your library. In the iPhone Settings app, tap ArtCurato, tap Photos, and choose Full Access so it can look through your whole library. (When the app first asks, the same choice is labeled Allow Full Access.) With limited access it can only see the photos you hand-pick.

Are my photos safe? Yes. The app reads your library on your device to organize it. It never edits, deletes, moves, or uploads your photos. See our Privacy Policy and Age Suitability.

How do I start over? Settings › "Clear and Reset My Curation" clears the app's organization and leaves your photo library untouched.
