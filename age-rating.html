ArtCurato Age Suitability

Last updated: September 28, 2026

ArtCurato is made for general audiences. It turns the art and places already in your own photo library into a quiet, private gallery. There is nothing social in it: no chat, no feed, no accounts, and nothing from other people.

What ArtCurato does not have

  • No messaging, chat, comments, or social feed.
  • No content from other users. Everything personal in the app comes from your own photo library.
  • No accounts or sign-in.
  • No advertising and no in-app purchases.
  • No gambling, contests, or loot boxes.
  • No location tracking. The app reads only the location already saved in your photos, to group them by place.

What you will see

  • Your own photos. ArtCurato shows photos from your library that it recognizes as art or places worth keeping. It only reads your library; it never edits, uploads, or shares a photo on its own.
  • Famous works of art. The "Masterpiece of the Day" and museum highlights feature well-known paintings and sculptures from museum collections, most of them in the public domain. As in any art museum, some of these works depict the human figure, including classical nudity (for example, Michelangelo's David or Botticelli's The Birth of Venus). They are presented as fine art, with the title, artist, and museum, never in a sexual context.
  • Museum information. Museum descriptions and banner images come from public sources such as Wikipedia and the museum's own website.

Links that leave the app

Some screens link to a museum's website or a Wikipedia article. These open in Apple's in-app Safari view. The pages themselves come from those websites, not from ArtCurato, and Safari's Screen Time content restrictions apply to them.

If you choose to identify a photo or share it, iOS's standard share sheet opens and you decide where it goes. This only happens when you tap to do it.

Privacy

ArtCurato does not collect personal information from anyone, including children. There are no analytics and no trackers. Read the full Privacy Policy.

For parents

  • The app needs access to the photo library to work. iOS lets you give it access to all photos or only selected ones, and you can change that anytime in Settings > Privacy & Security > Photos.
  • Screen Time can limit web content in the in-app Safari view and set time limits for the app.
  • The App Store listing shows ArtCurato's current age rating.
